What is a futures trade copier, and how does it work?

A futures trade copier is software that uses activity in a designated leader account to place corresponding orders in one or more follower accounts. It can reduce repetitive order entry when you manage multiple futures accounts, but the orders still depend on each account's broker, margin, permissions, connection status and available liquidity.

Leader and follower accounts explained

You place a futures trade in your leader account. The copier detects that activity and routes matching or resized orders to the followers you've enabled. A copier does not turn multiple prop firm accounts into one account: each account retains its own margin requirements, drawdown limits, open positions and order-rejection risk.

Example: copying with a smaller follower size

Suppose the leader fills two micro futures contracts. With a 1:1 configuration, a follower would normally receive an order for two. With a 0.5 multiplier, the intended follower quantity would be one, provided the software supports that sizing rule and the order is permitted. Not every follower will necessarily fill at the same price—or fill at all.

How to set up futures trade copying

1. Confirm broker and prop firm compatibility

Check the actual broker or datafeed connection behind every account. A trading front-end and the underlying connection are not always the same thing. Also review the firm rules covering automated order placement, copying, account ownership, maximum positions and consistency.

2. Link supported accounts

Connect the account or login that you will trade from and the follower accounts that should receive orders. TradeLink connects futures accounts through a Tradovate login, and Rithmic is coming soon. Always check current integration availability before paying. If all your accounts are on Tradovate, see our Tradovate trade copier guide for how Tradovate's built-in Group Trade compares with a cloud copier.

3. Configure copying groups and quantities

Choose a leader and which followers are active. Then choose suitable sizing such as 1:1, fixed quantity or a multiplier, where supported. Smaller funded accounts may need more conservative sizes than larger evaluation accounts.

4. Set protections and test small

Review per-account or per-group risk rules, available stop controls and how the platform handles partial fills and rejected orders. Start with a small supervised test in an appropriate environment before relying on a large copying group.

5. Monitor every follower account

Watch open positions and order status in the broker account as well as the copier. Latency, disconnected accounts, broker rejections and slippage can create divergence. A copier is not a replacement for risk oversight.

Frequently asked questions

Is using a futures copier the same as copying another trader?

No. A self-directed copier typically routes your own leader-account trades to your own follower accounts. Copying another person's signals or trades is a different workflow with different permissions and risks.

Can different futures accounts use different quantities?

Many copiers support fixed sizes or multipliers. Your software must support the selected mode, and your broker must accept the resulting orders.

Are copied futures orders guaranteed to match?

No. Connection issues, margin limits, rejects, partial fills, liquidity and slippage can all produce different results across accounts.

Is copying permitted at every prop firm?

No universal rule applies. Requirements can differ across firms and plans, and they can change. Verify your firm agreement before enabling copying.
